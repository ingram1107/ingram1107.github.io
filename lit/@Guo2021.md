Stated by the authors, car following models are used to study homogeneous
traffic flow characteristics. These models include Krauss's model, IDM model,
Wiedemann's model, and BKerner's model.

Krauss model, explained by the authors, is a microscopic, space-continuous car
following model that is established based on safe speed, $V_{\text{safe}}$. Safe
speed is calculated as follows:

$$
V_{\text{safe}} = V_l(t) + \frac{g(t) - V_l(t) \times T_r}{\frac{V_l(t) + V_f(t)}{2b} + T_r}
$$

**Notations**:
- $V_l(t)$ is the leading vehicle's speed
- $V_f(t)$ is the following vehicle's speed
- $b$ is the maximum deceleration of the vehicle

Safe speed can be defined with another formula written as follows:

$$
V_{\text{safe}} = -bT_r + \sqrt[b^2 T_r^2 + V_l^2 + 2bg]
$$

Krauss's model is not without errors. Often time, as claimed by the authors, its
acceleration and deceleration process would change suddenly due to the
acceleration reach the maximum value at the next step.

SUMO improved the model with the following formula:

$$
V_{\text{safe}}^2 = 2b \{S + (V_l T_i - \frac{bT_i^2}{4}) + \frac{(V_l - \frac{bT_i}{2})^2}{2b} - [(T_r + T_i)V_{\text{safe}} + \fraco{bT_i^2}{4} \}
$$

**Notations**:
- $S$ is the distance between the following vehicle and the leading vehicle in
  metre
- $T_i$ is the time required to accelerate from 0 to maximum in seconds
- $\frac{V_l T_i - bT_i^2}{4}$ is the displacement of the leading vehicle which
  is from the beginning of braking to maximum acceleration
- $\frac{(\frac{V_l - bT_i}{2})^2}{2b}$ is the displacement of the leading
  vehicle which is from the maximum acceleration to stop
- $(T_r + T_i) V_{\text{safe}} + \frac{bT_i^2}{4}$ is the displacement of the
  following vehicle in the driver's reaction stage and acceleration phase

From the above formulae, we can find the safe speed, $V_{\text{safe}}$ with the
below equation:

$$
V_{\text{safe}} = -b(T_r + T_i) + \sqrt[b^2(T_r + T_i)^2 + 2bS + (V_l + \frac{bT_i}{2})^2]
$$

Generally speaking, stated by the authors, the following vehicle's speed will be
less than the safe speed and maximum speed on the road. Thus, we can define the
desired speed as:

$$
V_{\text{desired}} = \text{min}[V_{\text{max}}, V_{\text{safe}}, V + a\tau]
$$

**Notations**:
- $a$ is the acceleration of the vehicle
- $\tau$ is the step duration of the simulation
