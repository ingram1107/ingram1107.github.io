## Overview

VANET, defined by Zhou, is a special category of Ad-hoc network consisting of
moving vehicle nodes in various scenarios such as highway or urban area.

VANET is a core part of ITS. It used DSRC 802.11p and later on adapted to
LTE-V2X PC5 interface Mode 4. It has several applications, including safety
message dissemination, route planning and detour, intelligent traffic light and
road crossing, and infotainment extension.

VANET is a share medium network.

The most fundamental when designing a VANET architecture are [Physical Layer](../202206131647.md)
and [Data Link Layer](../202206131651.md) (MAC layer in the author's word). Both
of them define the basic channel access methods and medium sharing mechanism.
There is no defined routing protocol in [Network Layer](../202206131702.md) in
DSRC 802.11p standard. 3GPP standard defined LTE-V2X side link mode 3 and 4,
where mode 4 is V2V without coordination of cell tower.

When designing a routing protocol for VANET, there are several considerations.
The author stated there are *route established speed, bandwidth cost, bandwidth
utilisation, resilience capacity to link failure, routing overhead, link recover
speed, and route maintenance*.

**Note**: Proactive based routing protocol are table driven.

Routing protocol has to be standardised for future VANET products like OBU to
communicate with each other using common functions. As Zhou proposed, they have
to be certified under regulations to ensure compatibility to avoid performance
degradation and severe safety issue.

## Scenario Analysis

Under **high relative movement and topology change**, communication is greatly
affected by Doppler spread under **high relative vehicle speed**. This is common
in highway scenario. The behaviour of vehicles on highway are categorised into
two types, which are low relative speed (except opposite highway lanes) and high
density (during congestion or accidents).

The relative speed on the same highway is ranged within 0 - 30 mph (48 km/h). In
this case, a vehicle node would stay within transmission range of another at
least for 2 to 5 minutes, long enough to establish route. However, there are
more nodes on the highway, topology will change more frequently and breakage
will be more often.

**Note**: Topology is dynamic due to frequent lane merge and passing.

**Note**: The communication range of VANET (using DSRC) is less than 1 km.

Vehicles on the opposite lanes will have high relative speed of around 130 mph
or 209 km/h. Active link period only has about 15 seconds. Some packets
broadcast will be carried by passing nodes and rebroadcasted to far unrelated
zone due to the route request message broadcast. This increase the burden of
packet processing due to unrelated packets.

Ad-hoc routing protocol will assign TTL and compare it to hop count. The packet
will be discarded if the hop count exceeds TTL/max hop count. That being said,
as the number of nodes on highway increases, there will be more unnecessary
received packets, which will cause extra packet delay and collision. It is
better to confine broadcast region only on one side of highway where broadcast
node currently stay, which can be achieved by evaluating Doppler frequency shift
and compare with threshold value to determine if the packet is from the opposite
lane. The Doppler shift may be obtained by channel information contained in the
packet or [Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM)](../202404111037.md)
PHY layer sync words or through pilot carrier evaluation. Filtering out the
broadcast packets from the opposite lane will increase the utilisation rate of
on-board processing unit.

As in choosing a routing protocol for highway scenario, the author suggested
choosing the one that is sensitive to high dynamic movement.

In the urban scenario, node movement usually doesn't have high relative speed.
The average link active time is longer compare to highway cases, and the link
breakage happens between nodes is less frequent. However, since high dense
obstacles are common, signal is easily blocked and scattered due to its 5.9 GHz
high frequency (defined by DSRC).

Broadcast signal may be contained to a path along the clearance among buildings,
which matches that of ad-hoc protocols like *distance vector routing*. Obstacles
don't affect topology discovery process of such protocols as distance vector
routing working with received signal strength.

Some non-ad-hoc routing would fail if no information regarding obstacles on the
way to the next hop is provided. Greedy based routing algorithms like *Greedy
Perimeter Stateless Routing (GPSR)* route request process based on position
information of all neighbours to build topology. They also use
[shortest-path](../202204141149.md) for each hop to find the closest to the
destination. In the case of *location aided protocol (LAR)*, GPS or other
position techniques are required, usually with high precision clock. Dense
building area frequently resulted in below minimum RSSI GPS signal strength.

From analysing the cases of highway and urban environment, the author concluded
that it is essential to apply different routing protocols and communication
architecture under suitable scenarios.

## On DSRC

DSRC standard of WAVE has been merged to 802.11p standard. The standard provides
operational requirements for communication device, physical and [MAC layer](../202206131651.md)
requirement, channel assignment include its operational modes, and performance
requirement for vehicular environment.

802.11p collects modifications as revisions to corresponding 802.11/802.11a
physical or MAC clauses and programming interfaces. It is important for product
manufacturers to follow during software development or modification of existing
firmware.

DSRC is designated with 5.9 GHz band for V2V communication within line of sight
distance. Line of sight distance scenario implies in terms of fading model,
Rician distribution shall be used considering the existence of dominant line of
sight signal component. DSRC also defines on-board unit (OBU) and road-side unit
(RSU). Communication device can communicate on single channel or multiple
channels according to channelisation planning, which in DSRC ad hoc mode all
channels are used for communication.

**Note**: From Wikipedia, Rician distribution is the probability distribution of
the magnitude of a circularly-symmetric bivariate normal random variable,
possibly with non-zero mean (non-central).

DSRC physical layer employs OFDM and modulation methods such as BPSK, QPSK, and
16/64-QAM. Very High Throughput (VHT) mode, that is 256-QAM, is not used due to
its **high constellation error**, i.e., susceptible to noise, distortion, and
fading. Clear Channel Assessment (CCA) specification is employed in carrier
sensing. In DSRC standard, CCA is defined to indicate busy medium when detect
any valid OFDM transmission with received power equal to or greater than -85
dBm, corresponding to sensitivity of the minimum 3 Mbps transmission speed.

**Note**: The MAC addresses for DSRC OBU devices are randomly generated.

The default operation mode of channels in DSRC defined by aunique ad hoc mode
where basic service set identification (BBSID) is set to all zeros during
broadcast. There is no distributed beaconing mechanism and scanning function
implemented for ad hoc reason.

In the aspect of cross layer information exchange, MAC layer is allowed to issue
a high resolution RSSI request to physical layer to lock the automatic gain
control (AGC) and ready for high resolution mode of RSSI during communication.

The contention window (CW) size is fixed to avoid uncontrolled latency due to
oversize. Enhanced Distributed Channel Access (EDCA) can be employed to achieve
better [Quality of Service (QoS)](../202209282057.md) in MAC layer by more
flexible usage of different Inter-frame Spacing (IFS) to achieve QoS for
different service set. In 802.11p, EDCA requires an application dependant
designation of fix-size CW. Higher level of granularity for QoS makes resource
allocation more efficient.

DSRC defines that the packet error rate (PER) cannot exceed 10% for physical
layer service data unit (PSDU) length of 1000 bytes under 85 mph (136.79 km/h)
relative vehicular speed and PSDU length of 64 bytes under 120 mph (193.12
km/h). It also defines that PEr cannot exceed 10% for PSDU of 1000 bytes for
mandatorily supported data rate (3 Mbps, 6 Mbps, 12 Mbps) with the following
conditions:
- multipath delay spread within 400 ns time delay for a 5 seconds period for
  the same signal at receiver;
- maximum Doppler shift with absolute value of 2.1 kHz for 5 seconds period;
- 10 dB amplitude variation of at 100 Hz rate over a 5 seconds period;
- a simulated Rician channel with shape parameter K = 10.

### 802.11bd As Improvement

As an improvement to DSRC, 802.11bd is proposed. It is backward compatible and
interoperable with 802.11p, with its bandwidth upgraded to 20 MHz. It can employ
VHT with millimetre wave frequency range to utilise its capability on high
throughput. 802.11bd also uses LDPC, Space-Time Block Code (STBC), and MIMO.

802.11p utilises longer symbol period (double longer than 802.11a counterpart)
to better deals with multipath. This comes with a shortcoming, however, where at
higher Doppler speed, shorter sub-carrier space (156.25 kHz in 802.11p case)
tends to be more sensitive to carrier frequency shift. An improvement from
802.11bd is that it provides an option for 312.6 kHz sub-carrier spacing and
midambles to cope with fast varying channels. Midambles is inserted into the
front of each symbol for channel estimation under fast varying condition,
especially when LDPC is used in 802.11ax2X down-clock mode.

Dual Channel Modulation (DCM) is employed in 802.11bd to split user's channel
into 2 coherent and far separated channels for redundant message transmission.
One of them can choose either double the data rate by increasing the MCS order
or double the transmission duration. DCM provides more robustness towards
frequency selective fading.

802.11bd introduced a channel bonding technique to use 2 adjacent channels for
transmission when the demand for throughput needed. This technique can be used
together with EDCA (QoS). Another improvements brought by 802.11bd are Range
Extension (RE) and blind retransmission. The former increased transmission
power for better range coverage whereas the latter retransmit message whenever
needed. For the latter, the average gain is expected to increase due to
increasing chance of successful transmission acceptance.

## On LTE-V2X

LTE-V2X was proposed in 3GPP release 14 which contained operation mode 3 and
mode 4. Mode 4 (PC5 interface) is used for direct V2V communication without
participation of cell tower (eNodeB).

LTE-V2X utilised **Semi-Persistent Scheduling (SPS)**, which is used to
coordinate shared medium access among vehicles. SPS facilitates meta-stable
resource scheduling that acts on a demand basis. Scheduling can not only be
chosen for the next transmission, but also several consecutive ones controlled
by reselection counter. Rescheduling only occur when current resources don't
satisfy performance metrics for current transmission. Overall, SPS helps reduce
scheduling overhead and saves bandwidth for the network. That being said, due to
agnostic to demand event, resource scheduling distributed on even time spaced
reservation intervals which shows more unpredictable performance when message to
be sent has variable length and is generated aperiodically.

SPS incorporates *Resource Reservation Algorithm* to determine the best
candidate single subframe resource, which is a frequency-time chunk occupied 1
ms symbols with RBs covering one SCI and one or more than one TBs (Transport
Block). The algorithm determines the final pool of choice for all single
subframe candidate within current selection window, which is a time window that
can satisfy the current package transmission requirement. It takes into account
the Reference Signal Receiving Power (RSRP), reservation history and Received
Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) averaging over last 100 ms sensing window for
each candidate single subframe resource.

**Note**: Transport Block, or TB, contains actual data to be transmitted. It can
occupy several RBs. In face, depends on relative size to all RBs each
sub-channel contains, TB may occupy several sub-channels.

LTE-V2X adopted a resource allocation schema in [Physical Layer](../202206131647.md)
that has a basic resource unit categorised as *resource block (RB)*. It is a
block divided in 1 ms time slot that contains 14 [OFDM](../202404111037.md)
symbols. A 180 kHz frequency band contains 12 sub-carriers with each 15 kHz.
Multiple RBs constitutes a sub-channel.

**Physical Sidelink Control Channel (PSCCH)**, employed by LTE-V2X and occupies
2 RBs, is used to transmit Sidelink Control Information (SCI) which contains
meta information for data recovery.

**Physical Sidelink Shared Channel (PSSCH)** is responsible for transmitting
Transport Block (TB) which contains actual data, where each may occupy several
RBs. HARQ data retransmission mechanism is deployed on PSSCH.

**Physical Sidelink Broadcast Channel (PSBCH)** is used for broadcast
synchronisation information for sidelink.

### NR-V2X as improvement

NR-V2X was introduced within 5G-NR domain, looking for considerable improvement
on performance and reliability. The full standard is defined in 3GPP release 16.
The improvement comes from the aspect of coding, uplink modulation, MAC
control, and more flexible resource allocation scheme. Those improvements are
done with a goal to balance performance boost, energy efficiency and versatility
of application environment.

NR-V2X can utilise millimetre wave bands above 24 GHz with maximum bandwidth of
400 MHz, as well as frequencies below 6 GHz within maximum bandwidth of 100 MHz.
Compare to LTE-V2X's 20 MHz, this is a significant uplift.

There are two uplink and downlink access methods for NR-V2X: **OFDM** and
**Direct Fourier Transform Spread OFDM (DFT-s-OFDM)**. OFDM is optimal for high
[Throughput](../202304111957.md), while DFT-s-OFDM is for energy efficient and
capable device due to low Peak to Average Power Ratio (PAPR) and low requirement
on the device's linearity region. LTE-V2X did try to keep PAPR low by deploying
SC-FDMA, but this would result in lower throughput compare to OFDM.

NR-V2X introduced **Physical Sidelink Feedback Channel (PSFCH)** for efficient
feedback retransmission. PSFCH is used to improve the spectral efficiency and
reduce processing time and overhead. The last symbol of a slot is proposed to be
used as time domain resource for fast feedback. Channel State Information (CSI)
is considered to be placed in feedback channel but if payload is large then it
can be placed in PSSCH. Demodulation Reference Signal (DRS) evaluates the
channel quality (CQI) to select proper MCS scheme on best effort basis
especially for high density.

## Routing Protocols

All routing protocols used by VANET are ad-hoc in nature, which can be
categorised into three types: **table-driven (proactive), on-demand (reactive),
and location-based (position-based)**.

In the paper, the author explored some table-driven routing protocol like
*Destination Sequenced Distance Vectoring* and *Clusterhead Gateway Switch
Routing*.

For on-demand routing protocol, the author discussed *Dynamic Source Routing
(DSR)*, *Ad-hoc On-demand Distance Vector Routing (AODV)*, *Temporally-Ordered
Routing Algorithm (TORA)*, *Associativity-Based Routing (ABR)*, and *Signal
Stability Routing (SSR)*.

As for location-based routing protocol, the author explored on a single example
of *Greedy Perimeter Stateless Routing (GPSR)*.
