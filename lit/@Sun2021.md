Krauss model, as stated by the authors, is a microscopic space-continuous,
collision-free car following model. According to the theory of relative motion,
the constrained condition of collision avoidance of two vehicles is denoted as
safe velocity, $v_{\text{safe}}$. It is defined as follows:

$$
v_{\text{safe}} = v_l(t) + \frac{g(t) - v_l(t) \times \Gamma}{\frac{V_l(t) + v_f(t)}{2b} + \Gamma}
$$

**Notations**:
- $b$ is the maximum deceleration of the vehicle
- $\Gamma$ is the driver response time

The desired velocity of the vehicle, $v_{\text{des}}$, is defined as follows:

$$
v_{\text{des}} = \text{min}[v_{\text{max}}, v + at, v_{\text{safe}}]
$$

The authors claimed that Krauss model didn't consider the influence of the
following vehicle's velocity. The following vehicle may hardly catch up to the
desired velocity smoothly and presents a velocity steeping change phenomime
which is not allowed in simulation. Another shortcoming of the model is that it
only considers deceleration scene, as it is designed to avoid collision. This is
hard to express actual traffic characteristics.

The authors provided several improvements to the model.
