# High Availability (HA)

HA is a characteristic of a system that aims to ensure an agreed level of
*operational performance*, usually in the metrics of uptime, for a higher than
normal period. (cited from Wikipedia 2026) Cisco, 2026, defined HA as a
capability of "an IT system, component, or app" that "can operate at a high
level, continuously, without intervention, for a given time period."

HA is a property of [network resilience](202607261053.md). A HA system should
continue operating despite either simple miss configuration, large scale natural
disasters, or even targeted [cyberattack](202408141950.md). It is configured
to deliver *quality performance* and handle different loads and failures with
minimal or zero downtime.

It requires less human intervention to restore operation in a complex system as
most common cause for outages is due to human error.

A typical HA system should include services:
- supporting distributed processing
- supporting network storage
- maintaining service of communication services such as video conferencing,
  instant messaging, and online collaboration
- access to applications and data as needed

There are **3 principles** to help achieve HA, based on [Oracle's
recommendations](lit/@Oracle2023.md):
- elimination of *single points of failure*
    - by adding or building [redundancy](202607261118.md) into the
      system
- *reliable crossover*
    - as crossover tends to become single points of failure in
      redundant system
- *detection of failures* as they occur
    - by *continuous maintenance*

According to Cisco, a HA infrastructure should consist of four elements:
- [**Redundancy**](202607261831.md)
    - including hardware, software, application, and data redundancy
- **Replication**
    - data is replicated and shared with same nodes in the cluster
    - nodes must communicate with each other and share same information
- [**Failover**](202607261833.md)
    - the best practice is to have an off-premises failover system
- [**Fault Tolerance**](202607261841.md)

HA is often measured in the numbers of "nines", see more in
[Availability](202210022157.md).

The best practices for HA are as follows:
1. Monitoring of the relevant components
2. Requirements and procurement
3. Operations
4. Avoidance of network failures
5. Avoidance of internal app failures
6. Avoidance of external services that fail
7. Physical environment
8. Network redundancy
9. Technical solution of backup
10. Process solution of backup
11. Physical location
12. Infrastructure redundancy
13. Storage architecture redundancy
